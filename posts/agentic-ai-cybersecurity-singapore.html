Agentic AI Revolution: The New Frontier of Cybersecurity Threats in Singapore

Date: February 26, 2026

Reuben Koh, a cybersecurity expert in Singapore, knows when he's watching artificial intelligence attack a network—the patterns look all wrong. "We know that AI agents are being utilised in this manner because no human would do it that way," he told Tech in Asia. This chilling observation marks a fundamental shift in the cybersecurity landscape, as agentic AI transforms how cyberattacks are conceived, executed, and defended against.

The Evolution from Human-Led to AI-Driven Attacks

For decades, coordinated cyberattacks have been slow and methodical, unfolding over days or weeks. Attackers would gain initial access, then move carefully through networks, trying to avoid detection. Each step required human decision-making, planning, and execution. Those days are rapidly becoming obsolete.

Agentic AI represents a paradigm shift in cyber threats. Unlike traditional automated attacks that follow predetermined scripts, AI agents can now handle almost every aspect of a cyberattack autonomously—from reconnaissance and vulnerability scanning to payload delivery and exfiltration—with humans stepping in only for strategic decisions. The speed and sophistication of these attacks are unprecedented, with AI-powered intrusions executing in hours or minutes what previously took weeks.

Why Traditional Defenses Are Failing

The challenge for cybersecurity professionals in Singapore and beyond is that AI-driven attacks don't behave like human attackers. Traditional detection systems are calibrated to identify human attack patterns—the slow lateral movements, the careful avoidance of detection, the predictable escalation of privileges. Agentic AI operates differently, exploiting vulnerabilities at machine speed and adapting its tactics in real-time based on what it learns about the target environment.

"The patterns are all wrong," explains Koh. "AI agents don't follow the playbook that security systems are designed to detect. They don't get tired, they don't make mistakes out of impatience, and they can simultaneously pursue multiple attack vectors without the cognitive limitations that constrain human attackers."

Fighting Fire with Fire

Singapore's cybersecurity experts are responding with a controversial but increasingly necessary strategy: fighting AI with AI. Organisations are deploying their own agentic AI systems to detect, respond to, and neutralise AI-powered threats in real-time. This arms race between offensive and defensive AI is reshaping the cybersecurity industry.

The approach involves deploying AI security agents that can autonomously monitor network traffic, identify anomalies indicative of AI-driven attacks, and take immediate action to contain threats. These defensive AI systems can correlate millions of security events across an organisation's infrastructure, identifying attack patterns that would be impossible for human analysts to detect.

Singapore's Position in the AI Security Arms Race

Singapore is uniquely positioned to lead in this new era of AI cybersecurity. The country's Advanced Research and Technology (ARTE) cluster, combined with its status as a regional technology hub, has attracted significant investment in AI security research. The Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA) has been proactively engaging with industry partners to develop guidelines for responsible AI deployment in security applications.

Local cybersecurity startups are also stepping up. Companies like Singapore-based SentinelOne and regional players are developing AI-native security platforms that leverage agentic AI for threat detection and response. The Singapore government's Smart Nation initiative provides a testbed for these technologies, with critical infrastructure serving as a proving ground for AI-driven security solutions.

The Road Ahead

As agentic AI becomes more sophisticated, the boundary between offensive and defensive AI capabilities will continue to blur. Organisations in Singapore must grapple with difficult questions about how to protect themselves against AI-powered threats while navigating the ethical implications of deploying autonomous AI security systems.

One thing is clear: the status quo is no longer viable. Traditional perimeter-based security approaches are insufficient against AI-powered attacks that can adapt, learn, and evolve. The future of cybersecurity in Singapore and globally will be defined by how effectively organisations can harness agentic AI for defense—or risk being overwhelmed by those who wield it for attack.

The message from experts is unambiguous: the time to prepare for an AI-driven threat landscape is now. Those who delay risk finding themselvesdefenceless against adversaries operating at the speed of thought—or faster.
